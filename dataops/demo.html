Demo 2 · Auto-Ingestion Safety · Earned Trust

”The $420K Load Your Agent Approved”

Context — what your agent did last quarter
Tuesday, 02:17 AM. Your data quality agent auto-approved a load from Airflow Pipeline #47. The source had been returning stale cached data since Monday’s infra update. Three downstream pipelines ingested it. Finance ran Q3 close on it.

The trust dimensions the gate watches:
  • Data quality — is the data accurate, complete, and fresh?
  • Ingestion latency — is the pipeline delivering within SLA? A source that delivers accurate data 6 hours late is not trustworthy.
  • Throughput stability — has load volume spiked or dropped unexpectedly?
  • Freshness SLA — when was the last verified load? Staleness = risk.
Who cares: CDOs deploying agents without guardrails on quality OR performance
What’s at stake: $420K in Q3 restatement costs. CFO asks “who approved this?”

Run: the same load, with and without the Conservation Gate

What the trust scores reveal. Each source earns its trust from verified decisions — not from a configuration file. After 500 decisions, SAP S/4HANA has earned 0.94, dbt Cloud 0.82, Celonis 0.71 — all above the θ_min threshold of 0.45, safe for autonomous approval. Airflow #47 sits at 0.23. Manual XLS at 0.14. Both below the line. When the agent tries to auto-approve the Airflow load at 2 AM, the Conservation Gate runs the θ_min check, it fails, and the decision routes to a human. Four seconds. The engineer rejects the stale data. The correct load runs at 6:30 AM. Cost avoided: $420K in Q3 restatements.

Measured result · Conservation Gate theorem

θ_min = 23.53/(α×V). The Conservation Gate runs a formal check before every autonomous decision. In production: 9,826 gate checks, zero violations. The gate does not approve unless the system has earned enough verified history in that decision category. This is a theorem, not a threshold.

Why you should care: Every CDO is being asked to deploy agents. The question isn’t whether to — it’s how to prevent the $420K Tuesday. The Conservation Gate is mathematically bounded: it cannot approve what the system hasn’t earned. That’s the difference between “automation” and “earned autonomy.”

Demo 6 · Automation Governance · Lifecycle Management

”The Deployment Rule That Demoted Itself”

Context — what happens after you deploy automation

Your team automated a triage rule: “auto-resolve partition-count alerts.” It worked for weeks — 93% accuracy through decision 30. Then the infrastructure changed. Partition semantics shifted. The rule started getting it wrong. Nobody noticed for 20 more decisions.

Who cares: CDOs who’ve been burned by automation that silently degrades
What’s at stake: the trust your team places in any automated rule

Run: the full lifecycle of one automation rule

What the lifecycle shows. The rule “auto-resolve partition-count alerts” is discovered by AgentEvolver, shadow-tested on live decisions, and promoted at decision 30 with 93% accuracy. Then the infrastructure changes. Partition semantics shift. Accuracy drops — 89%, 85%, 82% — crossing below the 78% correctness floor at decision 42. The system demotes its own rule. Alerts return to manual triage. The full audit trail shows exactly when it was promoted, what it was tested against, and why it was pulled. No human had to notice. No incident had to happen first.

Measured result · AgentEvolver production data

~10 rules tested, ~7 rejected. The system’s restraint rate IS the credibility proof. Every rule has a full audit trail: when it was discovered, what it was tested against, when it was promoted, why it was demoted. The 70% rejection rate means the system is strict enough to trust.

Why you should care: Your current automation doesn’t have a demotion mechanism. Once a rule is deployed, it stays deployed until someone notices it’s wrong. RGI’s AgentEvolver catches degradation before it becomes an incident — and has the audit trail to prove it.

”3-Way Match Fix — 38 min to 5 min”

Context — Celonis shows WHERE. Who explains WHY? Who fixes WHAT?

Your process mining dashboard says: the 3-way match (PO → GR → Invoice) takes 38 minutes, 80% of total cycle time. Celonis shows the bottleneck: the vendor matching step. It cannot tell you why. And it cannot reach into SAP to fix it.

The fix gap:

  • Process mining identifies the slow step — cannot trace data lineage to root cause
  • Your team: 2–4 hours to trace to the SAP configuration that caused it
  • RGI’s Tech-Process Fusion: names the root cause AND generates the SAP-specific fix
Who cares: CDOs who’ve bought process mining and still can’t close the loop to a fix
What’s at stake: $180K/month in delayed invoice processing, supplier payment penalties

Run: decompose the bottleneck — where the 38 minutes go

What the waterfall reveals. Celonis says the 3-way match takes 38 minutes, 70% of total cycle time. The waterfall decomposes those 38 minutes: Data Fetch (1 min), Vendor Match (35 min), Validation (1 min), Write-back (0.5 min), Queue (0.5 min). The bottleneck is one stage — the join. Tech-Process Fusion connects the process layer to the data layer and names the cause: a quarterly vendor master consolidation merged 3 regional supplier tables, tripling join cardinality on LIFNR. The recommended fix is a partition filter on BUKRS (company code) (87% confidence, based on 3 similar past fixes). After the fix: 38 minutes → 5 minutes.

SAP process fix recommendation
  • Root cause: Vendor master consolidation — 3 regional supplier tables merged into global LIFNR table, join cardinality tripled
  • Immediate fix: partition filter on BUKRS (company code) before vendor join — cardinality back to pre-consolidation levels. 3× → 1×
  • SAP config: SE16N → table LFA1 → secondary index on LIFNR + BUKRS. 15-min deploy, no transport in sandbox
  • Preventive rule: AgentEvolver monitors future vendor master consolidations — any >2× cardinality increase triggers auto partition filter recommendation
Measured result · Cross-system data lineage trace

Celonis shows: 3-way match = 38 min, 80% of total. RGI explains: vendor master consolidation merged 3 regional tables, tripling LIFNR join cardinality. The fix is a partition filter on BUKRS — based on 3 similar past consolidations, 87% confidence. Fix applied: 38 min → 5 min.

Why you should care: Process mining shows you the WHAT. Your team still has to figure out the WHY. Tech-Process Fusion connects the process layer to the data layer — so when a vendor consolidation causes a bottleneck, the system traces the data lineage to the consolidation, not just the slow step.
