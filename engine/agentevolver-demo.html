RGI AgentEvolver

A production deployment that rewrites itself at runtime — reversibly, and under a conservation gate

The CI platform has three layers: a context graph that holds cross-system knowledge, a Graph Attention Engine that scores decisions with calibrated geometry, and six decision loops that compound with every verified outcome. AgentEvolver is the loop that adapts deployed configurations at runtime — thresholds, factor weights, process routing — through a six-step lifecycle: detect, propose, shadow-test, gate, promote or reject, and monitor. If the evidence isn't there, it does nothing.

Demo 8 · S2P · Adversarial Resilience

"A Compromised Supplier Submits 200 Invoices"

Context — coordinated manipulation across three systems
A compromised supplier submits 200 invoices with manipulated line items over 6 weeks. Goal: shift the system's learned thresholds so future overcharges pass silently. Supply-chain fraud: $42B/year (ACFE).

The manipulation is invisible in any single system. Each invoice passes SAP validation. The process variant looks normal in Celonis. Line-item amounts are within range in Snowflake. The pattern emerges only when you correlate across all three: month-end timing clusters × process variant shifts toward auto-approve × line-item distribution skewing 8–12% above mean.

Run: toggle each defense layer to see its contribution

Measured result

57% → 14% non-recovery. Three-layer defense: asymmetric η (dampens bad overrides) + conservation pause (stops learning when signal degrades) + AgentEvolver rollback (reverses per-category poisoning). Cross-system detection via CGA on SAP + Celonis + Snowflake graph. Synthetic, controlled. Average data-poisoning incident: $3.8M (IBM Cost of a Data Breach).

Product · DataOps dashboard — cross-system detection: SAP × Celonis × Snowflake
CIO question answered: "What happens when our data is deliberately wrong?" — A fine-tuned model can't un-learn one batch of bad data. This system reverses per-category poisoning instead of carrying it forward. The defense is layered — and the layers are complementary, not redundant.
