Graph engineering, typed machine decisions (jev), and recursive self-improvement are all moving fast — and all reaching for the same missing capability: an enterprise system that doesn't just act, but reasons better over time, and knows when not to decide. Where RGI separates

what it is — and is not

A category is defined by what it excludes. Three fields are reaching for the same capability; RGI is the one that compounds and stays governed. The membership test makes the boundary exact:

The test — a system is RGI iff
(1) it reshapes the decision boundary — the surface that assigns actions — from verified outcomes of its own decisions; and (2) that reshaping is conservation-governed, with the decision procedure an invariant the learning cannot alter. The two clauses exclude the incumbents by construction.
rung below vs. Graph engineering — read & route
Graph engineering
A knowledge graph retrieves a cited path; a graph of agents routes work across a topology. Both are real advances — and both leave the graph as plumbing: structure the model reads or routes through. The reasoning happens once, then is discarded. Decision ten-thousand is decision one.
RGI
The graph reasons. The action is scored as geometry, and every verified outcome reshapes that geometry — so decision ten-thousand is measurably sharper than decision one, on your decisions. Read and route are structure; only reshape compounds.
the dangerous cousin vs. Recursive self-improvement (RSI / rrsi)
RSI
Recursion over the self — the system rewrites its own weights and code to get smarter. Unbounded, opaque, and the self-poisoning vector the field itself now flags. No risk owner approves automation that can silently change its own decision logic — which is why it stays in the lab.
RGI
Recursion over the judgment graph — the decision boundary — under a conservation gate. Bounded, inspectable, reversible. It deliberately does not rewrite itself; the procedure a risk owner approved is an invariant. RGI is the governed, deployable instance of recursive improvement.
the fast layer vs. Typed machine decisions (jev)
jev
A fast typed decision — a probability back in milliseconds, the model's own opinion, the same every time. Then it forgets. Stateless: decision ten-thousand is handled exactly like decision one.
RGI
The compounding memory around the fast decision: it scores against verified-outcome prototypes, gates the risky calls, and gets better. Wrap jev as the millisecond scorer if you like — RGI is the verified-outcome memory underneath it.

The substrate — GAE & the math

what the loop runs on

The loop comes alive on a substrate: one shared Graph Attention Engine (GAE) — ~844 parameters of judgment geometry — and the math beneath it. Every decision is two operations, score and learn, repeated under the conservation gate. The engine is the open-source core; the geometry is what compounds. The two operations, made precise:

Score
a*(v) = argmin_a ‖v − μ[c,a]‖²_DK  ·  p = softmax(−d²/τ)
the action is the nearest prototype — a point you can name
Learn
μ ← μ + η·y·(v − μ)  (η = 0.05 confirm / 0.01 override)
one verified outcome moves one centroid — auditable, reversible
Gate
α·q·V ≥ θ_min  ·  θ_min = 23.53/(α·V)
act only once earned — three layers: absolute, drift, rate

RGI · Compounding Intelligence, from the enterprise to the edge

Recursive Graph Improvement (RGI) is the reasoning layer of the agentic stack: a judgment graph whose decision boundary reshapes from verified outcomes while the decision procedure stays fixed and governed — the bounded, deployable instance of recursive improvement. Named operations are score / learn / gate under the conservation law α·q·V ≥ θ_min, with an inspectable centroid state. Figures labelled Measured are controlled experiments on synthetic streams with known ground truth; the sparse-adversarial detection rate is a characterized sensitivity boundary, not a failure. No production customers yet. Process/ERP reification, structural self-computation, and the full cross-copilot control plane are roadmap, not shipped.
