Job 1 · order it

Order Autopilot — runs your food order like your best GM, and asks when it's unsure

Problem

Your order guide is last spring's pars — your GMs override it or spend an hour a day rebuilding it, and one strange weekend still buries the walk-in or 86s your movers. Now multiply that by every unit, every day.

What it does

Builds and places the food & bev order itself — order / more / less / skip per category, tuned to this store's covers, weather and events — and takes over category by category only as it earns it.

RGI edge

A static order guide can't adapt and a recommend-only tool still needs a hand on every line. RGI is the one that places the order and hands back only the calls it hasn't earned — ordering you can actually leave on.

Job 2 · use it

Usage Truth — bought vs. should-have-used vs. actually-used

Problem

You know what you bought — the invoices are right there. You don't know, by item by store, what reached the plate vs. what died in the walk-in or got over-portioned. The P&L shows the gap a month late, as one blurry point of food cost.

What it does

Lines up three numbers your systems keep apart — bought, should-have-used (sold × recipes), and actually-used (counts) — and names the item and the store where the gap lives.

RGI edge

Inventory suites record what happened; RGI closes the loop to what should have happened and acts on the gap — the half of food cost your invoices and your POS each only half-see.

Job 3 · check what showed up

Spec Watch — catch a vendor's quiet yield change before the P&L does

Problem

A distributor quietly shrinks a pack, swaps a cut, or the case yield drops a few points. Your food cost creeps across a dozen stores and nobody can say why until month-end — if ever.

What it does

Tracks the realized yield of each item — usable product per case after trim and spoilage — across every store, and when it drifts below its learned norm names the item, the vendor and the week.

RGI edge

Nobody watches yield across stores — the leading indicator lives in your kitchens, not on an invoice. RGI catches the drift weeks before the P&L, and before the vendor admits it.

Job 4 · check what you paid

Invoice Integrity — decompose the price move your contract didn’t catch

Problem

Your 11-year distributor quotes +14%, "driver shortages." It clears as "the market" — because it's the vendor you trust most — and the overcharge disappears into a food-cost point nobody can take apart.

What it does

Scores every invoice line against your contract and the learned norm, decomposes the increase — commodity market move vs. supplier markup — checks it against your contract ceiling, and drafts the credit claim.

RGI edge

Others store what you paid; RGI knows why it moved and whether your contract allows it — and claims the difference at the line, not at the P&L.

Job 5 · capture what's owed

Rebate Capture — the buying-group cashback you never file in time

Problem

You're in a buying group with cashback and rebates on lines you already buy — and a quarterly deadline you've never once hit, because matching SKUs and filing on time takes hours nobody has.

What it does

Matches your actual purchases to eligible SKUs and tiers, flags the in-group brand switches that capture cashback at no quality change, files before the window, and verifies the credit landed.

RGI edge

A rebate program is a live rule RGI holds and works, not a PDF in a drawer — and it learns which switches pay most, so capture compounds quarter over quarter.

Job 6 · protect it when prices (or demand) blow up

Margin Shield — protect food cost when a price — or a weekend — goes crazy

Problem

Eggs quadruple on bird flu, beef spikes, coffee blows up — or a heat wave plus a festival blows up a weekend. Most systems keep buying to par and you eat it, or you reprice a month too late.

What it does

When a volatile item's price (or the week's demand) breaks its normal range, it stops committing that item, switches to your backup spec, and flags which menu items to reprice or pull — then holds until it settles. On a demand shock it pauses the volatile categories back to your chef.

RGI edge

Every other system must always place an order. RGI is the only one that can say "trust me less right now" and cut its own exposure when the regime breaks — the capability nothing in the market has.

Job 7 · keep the know-how

Store Memory — open a new store seasoned; keep it when a GM walks

Problem

Every new store opens cold — the new GM over-orders for a quarter learning the room. And when a strong GM or chef leaves, the pars, vendor quirks and Friday instincts walk out the door. Across a chain, turnover and openings are a constant tax.

What it does

A new store opens already carrying what a comparable store learned — the pars, the vendor read, the daypart patterns — so the ramp is weeks not a quarter; and the judgment lives in the engine, so a departure doesn't reset the unit.

RGI edge

Rivals store order history and cost; RGI keeps the judgment and transfers it to a new store or a new GM. The continuity no competitor offers — turnover becomes a non-event.

Forward Buy — turn demand you're sure of into locked price and guaranteed supply

Problem

You order week to week and take whatever price and supply the market gives — a price-taker, exactly when prices swing most.

What it does

Because it knows how much of each ingredient you'll need and how sure it is, you commit the confident slice forward for locked price and guaranteed supply, and leave the uncertain rest to weekly buying.

The bottom line

Why Restaurant-RGI

The restaurant industry runs on 2–5% margins. Food cost is the largest controllable line — and every tool in the market runs Day-365 logic identical to Day 1.

It acts

Places the order, catches the yield drift, claims the overcharge, files the rebate. Every other tool recommends; this one closes the loop.

It knows when not to act

Cuts its own exposure when eggs quadruple or a weekend blows up. The conservation law gives it a mathematical floor; no other platform can do this.

It compounds

Every verified outcome from your kitchen sharpens the next decision. The gap against a static tool widens week over week and never narrows.

Why others can't

RGI learns from kitchen outcomes, not invoices. A learned decision geometry (140 values) reshapes from verified results; static tools have no feedback path from outcomes.
