The problem

Your AP team rejected 47 good invoices this quarter. Each one was a rule doing its job.

Procurement is a judgment problem wearing an optimization costume. Six pressures break the rule-and-queue status quo:

P1 · judgment

Rules are brittle

  • Thresholds fire on a number, not on why it moved
  • So they reject good invoices and wave maverick spend through
  • The judgment that'd get it right lives in a few experts' heads — and leaves when they do
P2 · non-stationary

The ground keeps moving

  • Commodity, tariffs, FX, supplier health, lead times shift weekly
  • Rules tuned in January are wrong by April
  • A model trained once decays in silence
P3 · non-linear

The hard calls interact

  • One PO carries price, demand, supplier, geo, alternative-availability and spend-pattern risk at once
  • No linear rule draws that boundary
  • Thresholds over- and under-fire on the calls that matter
P4 · scattered

Systems don't talk about cause

  • Invoice in the ERP, clause in the contract repo, price move in a commodity feed, process in the mining tool
  • Connectors move bytes; nothing links them into why
P5 · every call is a dollar

Wrong is asymmetric

  • Often the largest controllable spend line
  • Wrong auto-approval = a write-off; wrong rejection = a late supplier + maverick spend
  • At volume, getting it wrong breaks the firm
P6 · the agent gap

Today's agents draft, never graduate

  • Agentic-ERP fleets monitor, summarize, route for approval — and stop
  • Nothing measures earned authority, shows the CFO a ledger, or pauses when accuracy slips

What it will never do

Autonomy is earned per class and bounded. The dial is always visible, and the hard limits never move:

Routine reorder, on-contract
auto once the class is proven
Price anomaly
propose-first until earned
Sole-source / emergency

Restricted / sanctioned party
always holds — never auto

Illustrative: "41% auto-approved, here's the proof — 450 decisions, 91% blended, above the floor for eight weeks, and it pauses itself if accuracy drops." No vendor we've seen publishes that ledger.

The autonomy contract — per class, you set it
Accuracy floor
Auto-decides only while that class's audit-verified accuracy holds at or above the floor you set (default 90%).
Dollar ceiling
Auto only under a per-decision cap (default $50K); above it, policy escalates — human approval, then controller, then dual sign-off.
Pause & rollback
On a slip it reverts the class to propose-only and alerts the owner; every auto-decision is a reversible, signed ERP write in a 7-year ledger.
the full autonomy contract — floor, ceiling, verified-outcome lag, pause, rollback, record
Accuracy floor
The headline 91% is blended across auto + human-routed decisions; the auto-only slice is held to the stricter floor you set (default 90%).
Dollar ceiling
Per-decision cap (default $50K) and a per-day aggregate cap. Above it — >$50K → human approval, >$250K → controller, >$1M → dual sign-off.
Verified outcome — and its lag
Learns only from outcomes confirmed in SAP / the contract / the commodity index — price & 3-way-match in minutes–hours, goods-receipt & payment in days — plus a continuous 30% audit sample. Never from its own prediction.
What a pause does
Reverts the class to propose-only, alerts its named owner, flags recent auto-decisions for review. It re-earns autonomy only after verified accuracy climbs back above the floor on a fresh window.
Rollback
Every auto-decision is a reversible, signed write to the ERP with the prior state kept — one click to reverse, and the reversal is itself logged.
On the record
Every decision, pause and reversal lands in a 7-year signed ledger (SOX / EU-AI-Act-shaped), under the oversight chain you approved — analyst → category manager → controller.
