The problem

You gave back Q3's gains in Q4 — and your journal can't tell you why

Every tool helps you execute; none computes over your verified outcomes to tell you how you actually trade.

  • Everything is static — TradingView charts, Alpaca/IBKR fill, your journal records; trade #10,000 scored like trade #1
  • You can't prove what you suspect — your most-traded setup underperforms your average, while a setup you rarely take quietly beats it
  • Size jumps 40% after three winners · Friday 2–4pm Sharpe 0.3 vs 1.8 overall — all buried in monthly P&L
  • The journal always has an insight — so none can tell you when you're genuinely clean

Four structural limits of the current stack — architecture gaps, not feature gaps:

1 · Record, don't compute

Journals store P&L by setup. signal truth None compute per-signal, outcome-conditioned variance — which indicator is signal and which is noise for you.

2 · Model the strategy, not the trader

Backtests assume perfect execution. proof to scale They can't prove a strategy is safe to scale on your real fills and exits.

3 · Static scoring

No verified outcome feeds back. fingerprint Day 365 runs the same logic as Day 1 — your edge drifts and nothing notices.

4 · Always an answer

No abstention, no competence boundary. proof gate steps back Can't certify "clean," can't cut its own autonomy when the regime breaks.

What you're probably thinking

"Doesn't Robinhood / Composer / TradingAgents already do this?"

They're agents that act — an LLM or rule set decides and places trades, on a generic model, and (Robinhood's own words) unsupervised. RGI is the layer that measures whether the action had verified edge, learned from your outcomes, and abstains when it didn't. Different job. You bolt RGI under an agent; the agent can't become RGI — it has no verified outcomes feeding per-signal trust, and it can't say "nothing here survives correction."

"If agents are the future, why observation-only?"

Because the thing the wave is missing — and the thing the Bank of England is trying to legislate with a kill switch — is exactly a competence boundary: knowing when to stop. An actor that always acts can't be that; the measurement layer that knows when the edge isn't there is. On the roadmap, that's the check an agent calls before it trades.

"If it's open source, what's defensible?"

The code is Apache 2.0 — fork it. Your ~200 learned values are your own portable record, not a lock-in. What's defensible sits elsewhere: the community factor library (network effect), the engine and its published math (the hard part), and the real answer — the moat is the five-copilot platform; Trading-in-the-open is its hardest-domain proof.

The question that sorts the field: "After 1,000 of my trades — which of MY signals have verified edge; does it ABSTAIN when nothing survives correction; does it cut its own autonomy when the regime breaks; from MY execution, on MY machine?" Every agent, journal and backtester answers no. That's the gap none of them fill.
